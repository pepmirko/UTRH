## 1. Scientific objective

The original strong UTRH states that disease/system-specific dynamics differ only by a scalar time factor,

\[
Q_{ds} = \frac{1}{t^*_{ds}} Q ,
\]

where \(Q\) is a shared generator and \(t^*_{ds}>0\) is a disease-system characteristic timescale.

The present plan does **not** claim that this strong statement is testable with the currently qualified public cellular corpus. The audit did not identify a dataset satisfying simultaneously:

1. at least two non-redundant anchors;
2. pairwise commensurability;
3. a genuine crossed disease × system design;
4. informative temporal coverage;
5. defensible initial conditions;
6. independent biological replication.

The purpose of this analysis plan is therefore to test **necessary and diagnostic consequences of scalar temporal rescaling** that are currently identifiable from public or recoverable data.

The central working formulation is:

> **Scalar temporal rescaling is treated as a conditional dynamical symmetry.**

The analysis asks both:

- where scalar rescaling is statistically adequate; and
- whether prespecified mechanisms predict where it breaks.

A successful local scaling result is not by itself evidence that the full generator-level UTRH is true.

# 2. Hierarchy of claims

## 2.1 Strong generator-level UTRH

For module \(m=(d,s,c)\),

\[
Q_m = \frac{1}{t_m^*} Q.
\]

This is the strongest claim and is **not directly tested** in the primary analyses below.

## 2.2 Distributional temporal scaling

For an event-time variable \(T_m\),

\[
T_m \overset{d}{=} t_m^* Z,
\]

for a common dimensionless random variable \(Z\).

Equivalent survival representation:

\[
S_m(t)=S_0(t/t_m^*).
\]

This is an Accelerated Failure Time-type scaling statement.

## 2.3 Shape invariance

If

\[
T_m=t_m^* Z,
\]

then all dimensionless shape statistics must be invariant across modules, including where estimable:

\[
CV_m = \frac{SD(T_m)}{E(T_m)},
\]

\[
R^{Q}_m = \frac{Q_{0.75}(T_m)}{Q_{0.25}(T_m)},
\]

and, for a stretched-exponential/Weibull family,

\[
S_m(t)=\exp[-(t/\lambda_m)^{\beta_m}],
\]

scalar rescaling permits \(\lambda_m\) to change but requires

\[
\beta_m=\beta^*.
\]

Shape invariance is therefore a necessary consequence of pure scalar rescaling.

## 2.4 Cross-level implication: cellular UTRH versus organism/population analogues

The strong UTRH of §2.1 is defined at the level of the cellular state process. The organism- or population-level analyses in P1–P3 are therefore **not direct tests of the cellular generator identity**.

A cellular scalar clock can imply scalar rescaling of an organism-level endpoint only under additional bridge assumptions that are fixed independently of the observed collapse:

1. the relevant cellular population shares the same module-level clock factor, rather than containing condition-dependent mixtures of cellular clocks;
2. the compared modules begin from aligned initial cellular-state distributions, or from an independently justified common entry state;
3. the organism-level event time is generated by the same time-homogeneous functional of the cellular trajectory in every compared module;
4. no independent clock—such as development, maturation, immune progression, exposure timing, or another organism-level process—contributes a condition-specific timescale to the endpoint;
5. the mapping from cellular state to the measured organism/population endpoint is invariant across modules.

Only when these premises are independently defensible does cellular scalar rescaling imply the corresponding organism-level rescaling. The converse implication is **never assumed**:

\[
\text{organism/population temporal scaling}\;\not\Rightarrow\;Q^{cell}_{ds}=Q^{cell}/t^*_{ds}.
\]

Accordingly, P1–P3 test temporal-scaling analogues or necessary consequences at their stated level. A failure at organism/population level counts against the strong cellular UTRH only when the bridge assumptions above are independently supported for that comparison; otherwise it is evidence only against the organism/population-level scalar model being tested.

# 3. Conditional temporal-scaling symmetry

Three candidate symmetry-breaking mechanisms are prespecified.

## C1 — Regime boundary

The data-generating mechanism changes across a severity, dose, temperature, repeat-length, age, or disease-stage boundary.

Prediction:

\[
M_1 \text{ may hold within regimes but fail across regimes.}
\]

## C2 — Phase-specific rate modifier

A modifier changes the velocity of only one phase of the path.

Prediction:

\[
t^*_{\mathrm{early}} \neq t^*_{\mathrm{late}}.
\]

A single global scalar is then inadequate.

## C3 — Mixture / frailty / subpopulation change

The observed module is a changing mixture of latent subpopulations with distinct kinetics.

Prediction:

- dimensionless shape statistics change;
- residuals become multimodal or overdispersed;
- a mixture/frailty model outperforms a single-scale model.

**Critical anti-immunization rule:** C1–C3 may be assigned only using mechanistic or design information that is independent of the observed success or failure of temporal collapse. They cannot be introduced retrospectively to rescue a failed fit.

# 5. Global analysis rules

## 5.1 No outcome-informed transformations

The following are prohibited in primary analyses:

- dataset-specific z-scoring chosen to improve collapse;
- vertical shifts fitted after inspecting agreement;
- arbitrary monotone warping;
- conversion of Cox hazard ratios into time-scale ratios;
- combining incompatible event definitions;
- redefining disease/system categories after inspecting results;
- exclusion of a module because it weakens the common-law fit unless the exclusion criterion was prespecified independently.

## 5.2 Identifiability

Only **relative** time scales are identifiable without an external reference.

For each fitted family, one reference scale is fixed:

\[
t^*_{\mathrm{ref}}=1
\]

or equivalently

\[
\sum_m \log t_m^*=0.
\]

The chosen normalization must be fixed before model comparison within a dataset.

## 5.3 Biological unit

Inference must be performed at the level of independent biological units appropriate to the source dataset.

Cells nested in the same donor, clone, culture, experiment, animal, or batch are not treated as independent biological replicates.

## 5.4 Missing data and censoring

Censoring is modeled explicitly.

A censored observation is never converted to an exact event time.

Grouped event/count data are analyzed through an interval/grouped likelihood, not by fabricating individual event times.

## 5.5 Multiple comparisons

Each primary analysis has one prespecified main comparison.

Secondary statistics are reported with uncertainty and are labeled exploratory unless explicitly designated otherwise below.

## 5.6 Evidence labels

Results are classified as:

- **supports adequacy of scalar scaling in this tested regime**;
- **inconclusive / underpowered**;
- **in tension with scalar scaling**;
- **rejects the specified scalar-scaling model for this dataset/regime**.

The phrases “proves UTRH” and “falsifies all UTRH” are prohibited.

# 6. Stage 0 — synthetic validation of the analysis code

Before analysis of biological data, the complete fitting pipeline must pass simulated-data unit tests.

## S0.1 Exact scalar scaling

Generate

\[
T_{im}=t_m^* Z_i
\]

from several baseline distributions:

- Weibull;
- log-normal;
- log-logistic;
- gamma where appropriate.

Add right censoring and interval censoring.

Requirement:

- recover relative \(t_m^*\) without systematic bias;
- do not spuriously infer shape differences;
- nominal uncertainty coverage should be approximately correct.

## S0.2 Shape violation

Generate modules with equal scale changes but different Weibull/stretched-exponential shape:

\[
\beta_1\neq\beta_2.
\]

Requirement: scalar model must be rejected or the alternative must receive clearly superior predictive support at prespecified effect sizes.

## S0.3 Phase-specific scaling

Generate piecewise trajectories with:

\[
a_{\mathrm{early}}\neq a_{\mathrm{late}}.
\]

Requirement: a single-scale model must not be reported as adequate when phase-specific models have materially superior held-out prediction.

## S0.4 Mixture/frailty

Generate two-component event-time mixtures whose mixture weights differ by module.

Requirement: changing shape/mixture must not be absorbed into a single \(t^*\).

## S0.5 Null pipeline test

Generate equal modules with no scale difference.

Requirement: the procedure must not systematically report a nonzero clock ratio.

## S0.6 Observation granularity

Generate exact scalar scaling

\[
T_{im}=t_m^*Z_i
\]

across modules spanning the full range of \(t_m^*\) expected in P2, discretise observations on scoring grids, and compute module-level mean and SD exactly as they would be computed from published summaries.

### S0.6a — Fixed common observation grid: identifiability stress test

Use one common grid spacing \(\Delta\) for all modules. Under exact scalar scaling, relative granularity

\[
g_m=\log(\Delta/\bar T_m)
\]

is expected to become strongly collinear with any rate modifier that monotonically determines \(t_m^*\). This subtest therefore does **not** require an adjusted estimate of \(\beta_1\) to be identifiable.

Requirements:

1. without the granularity term, the pipeline must detect that fixed-grid discretisation can induce a spurious association between \(\log CV_m\) and the rate modifier;
2. the §8.2 VIF rule must correctly flag the adjusted modifier effect as **NOT SEPARABLE FROM OBSERVATION GRANULARITY** when VIF > 5;
3. the pipeline must not report the resulting adjusted coefficient as evidence for or against scalar scaling.

Failure to flag this structural non-identifiability is a failure of S0.6a.

### S0.6b — Heterogeneous observation grids: adjustment validation

Repeat the simulation with \(\Delta_m\) varying across modules independently of the rate modifier, so that \(x_m\) and \(g_m\) are statistically separable in at least 70% of simulated datasets under the prespecified VIF ≤ 5 rule.

Requirements:

1. without the granularity covariate, the induced spurious modifier dependence must be detectable;
2. among simulations satisfying VIF ≤ 5, inclusion of \(g_m\) must restore nominal behavior: the adjusted \(\beta_1\) is compatible with zero and the false-positive rate at two-sided \(\alpha=0.05\) must not exceed 0.12 in this engineering validation;
3. if the separable fraction is < 0.70, the adjustment is considered insufficiently validated and P2a remains suspended pending redesign.

This amendment was triggered solely by synthetic-data engineering validation before biological outcome extraction. No biological result was inspected.

The biological analyses do not start until tests S0.1–S0.6 pass.

# 7. P1 — Jenkins2020 external AFT/shape benchmark

## 7.0 Benchmark source verification gate

Before any P1 analysis, the benchmark source must be verified against the checklist below and the result recorded in the raw numerical ledger.

1. full bibliographic identification (authors, year, journal, DOI, and PMID or PMCID) confirmed against the indexed record;
2. open-access route confirmed and full text obtained;
3. the survival source data and censoring representation located by exact table or supplementary-file reference;
4. at least one condition for which scalar temporal rescaling is reported to hold, identified by section and figure/table;
5. at least one condition for which it is reported to fail, identified by section and figure/table, with the reported basis for that failure;
6. biological-replicate structure documented.

**Pre-freeze verification status.** Jenkins et al. 2020, *eLife* 9:e56580, DOI 10.7554/eLife.56580, PMID 32690135, PMCID PMC7373428, satisfies the bibliographic/full-text gate. Independent source verification on 2026-09-24 confirmed that the article reports eight replicate experiments; Figure 5 source data are publicly linked; Supplementary file 5 contains the temporal-scaling/AFT analysis; Lip-1 and SIH reject simple temporal rescaling (reported p<0.01); and the 20 °C versus 25 °C temperature comparison corroborates simple temporal rescaling. The cellular feasibility audit separately records Figure 5 source data 1 and Supplementary file 5 as inspected, with age/alive/dead/censored fields. These source roles are fixed before re-analysis and cannot be reassigned after viewing P1 results.

If the data-level requirements above cannot be reproduced from the archived source files at execution time, Jenkins2020 is replaced rather than weakened. The prespecified fallback is the *C. elegans* Lifespan Machine corpus of Stroustrup et al. 2016 (*Nature* 530:103–107; DOI 10.1038/nature16550; PMID 26814965; PMCID PMC4828198). Its positive conditions are interventions/temperature ranges reported to temporally rescale; the prespecified negative condition is the cross-regime comparison at the ~30 °C transition, where the paper reports a deviation from temporal scaling. The fallback is a benchmark substitution only; it does not alter P2 or P3.

## 7.1 Purpose

This is a **pipeline-validation benchmark**, not evidence for neurodegeneration.

Jenkins2020 is used because the same study provides an empirical positive comparator (temperature-associated temporal rescaling) and treatment conditions reported to depart from temporal rescaling. P1 asks whether our implementation reproduces both directions without using them to tune downstream model choices.

## 7.2 Data object

The preferred input is the source workbook representation actually supplied by the study: age/interval, alive, dead and censored counts, preserving replicate identity and original observation intervals. Denote the grouped data generically as

\[
\mathcal D_J=\{t, n^{alive}, n^{dead}, n^{censored}, g, j\},
\]

where \(g\) indexes condition and \(j\) biological replicate.

No pseudo-individual exact event table will be generated from grouped counts.

## 7.3 Primary model

\[
M_{J1}: S_g(t)=S_0(t/a_g).
\]

Parameters:

- shared baseline survival \(S_0\);
- one relative scale \(a_g\) per intervention/condition;
- biological-replicate variation where supported by the source structure.

## 7.4 Alternative models

\[
M_{J2}: S_g(t)=S_{0,g}(t)
\]

with group-specific shape, or an explicitly parameterized family allowing a shape parameter to vary.

A flexible model may be used for diagnosis but cannot count as confirmation of scalar scaling.

## 7.5 Primary estimand

For each comparison:

\[
\Delta_g=\log a_g.
\]

Primary adequacy diagnostics:

- constancy of corresponding quantile ratios over the identifiable range;
- predictive calibration of the rescaled survival curve;
- residual-distribution diagnostics compatible with the grouped/censored likelihood.

## 7.6 Decision

Scalar scaling is considered **adequate for a benchmark condition** only if:

1. the prespecified scalar model has no major structured lack-of-fit;
2. corresponding quantile ratios are compatible with a constant within uncertainty;
3. held-out prediction is not materially improved by allowing group-specific shape.

At least one prespecified negative-control condition must fail at least one of these criteria. For Jenkins2020, Lip-1/SIH are the prespecified negative conditions; temperature is the positive comparator. If the fallback is invoked, the cross-regime ~30 °C comparison is the negative condition.

If the pipeline makes the prespecified positive and negative benchmark conditions appear equivalent, downstream biological analyses are suspended and the model/pipeline is revised before unblinding P2/P3 outcomes.

# 8. P2 — Prion shape invariance and rate-modifier analysis

## 8.1 Scope

This analysis will use only prion modules for which numerical summary statistics can be extracted without figure digitization.

Required minimum fields:

- strain;
- host/species/line;
- route;
- dose or PrP expression level where applicable;
- endpoint definition;
- mean incubation time;
- SD, or SEM plus \(n\);
- \(n\);
- scoring/observation interval (days between assessments), or an explicit statement that it is not reported;
- clinical scoring criteria and whether terminal endpoint was blinded;
- whether the reported dispersion is SD, SEM, range, or interquartile range.

If only SEM is reported:

\[
SD=SEM\sqrt{n}.
\]

Modules with ambiguous endpoint definitions are not pooled. Modules for which the scoring interval is not reported are excluded from the primary P2a analysis and reported as a separate sensitivity stratum.

## 8.2 P2a — Shape invariance

For each module:

\[
CV_m=\frac{SD_m}{\bar T_m},
\]

and

\[
y_m=\log CV_m.
\]

### Primary null

Within a prespecified compatible family of strain × host × route modules differing by the rate modifier:

\[
H_{P2a}:CV_m=CV^*.
\]

### Model

A random-effects meta-regression is used when enough independent modules exist:

\[
\log CV_m=\beta_0+\beta_1x_m+\beta_2g_m+u_m+\epsilon_m,
\]

where \(x_m\) is the prespecified rate modifier (for example log dose or PrP-expression category) and

\[
g_m=\log(\Delta_m/\bar T_m)
\]

is the log relative observation granularity, \(\Delta_m\) being the scoring interval. \(\beta_2\) is a prespecified nuisance covariate included unconditionally, not selected on the data.

Primary scale-symmetry prediction:

\[
\beta_1=0.
\]

If \(g_m\) is collinear with \(x_m\) beyond the prespecified threshold (variance inflation factor >5), \(\beta_1\) is declared **NOT SEPARABLE FROM OBSERVATION GRANULARITY** and P2a returns NOT IDENTIFIABLE for that compatible family under §14.

### Uncertainty

Uncertainty of \(CV\) must account for finite \(n\). If only summary statistics are available, no nonparametric subject-level bootstrap is permitted. Sensitivity analyses compare distributional approximations appropriate for positive event times (for example a normal approximation where justified versus a log-normal-based approximation).

### Minimum detectable effect

For design planning, the modifier is additionally expressed in standardized units, \(x_m^\dagger=(x_m-\bar x)/SD(x)\). Before outcome extraction, the anticipated design is conservatively defined as at least 12 independent compatible modules with typical group size \(n=5\)–10. Using the lower end (\(n=5\)) and the leading sampling-variance approximation \(Var[\log CV]\approx1/[2(n-1)]\), the prespecified minimum detectable standardized slope is

\[
\boxed{\beta_1^{\min}=0.30}
\]

on the log-CV scale at approximately 80% power and two-sided \(\alpha=0.05\). This corresponds to about a 35% multiplicative change in CV per 1-SD change in the modifier. This value is a design-resolution target, not a biological equivalence margin.

Before viewing P2a outcome estimates, the realised module count, group sizes and modifier spread are used to recompute the design-only MDE. If the realised MDE exceeds \(1.5\beta_1^{\min}=0.45\), P2a is reported as **UNINFORMATIVE BY DESIGN** and its point estimate is not interpreted in either direction. The same requirement applies to any shape statistic substituted for CV.

### Interpretation

A systematic dependence of dimensionless shape on the rate modifier after the granularity adjustment is evidence against pure scalar rescaling within that regime. Absence of such dependence is not informative if the design fails the MDE rule or enters the near-exponential triviality regime of §13.1.

## 8.3 P2b — Rate scaling

Where inoculum dose \(D\) is available:

\[
\log \bar T_m=\alpha-\gamma\log D_m+\eta_m.
\]

Primary estimand:

\[
\gamma.
\]

This analysis tests whether the extracted data reproduce a dose–incubation relationship. A source-independent reference is required for a true positive-control recovery claim.

### Interpretation of inoculum dose

Inoculum dose is prespecified as primarily a **seeding / initial-condition modifier**, not as a disease-module clock parameter in the strong cellular UTRH sense. Changing dose changes the amount or probability of initiating pathogenic material and therefore can alter the effective starting condition even if the downstream generator is unchanged. Consequently, P2b is a rate-relation / pipeline benchmark at the organism level. It is not interpreted as evidence that dose estimates or manipulates the UTRH parameter \(t^*_{ds}\), unless an independent mechanistic argument establishes aligned initial conditions and a global generator-rate change.

### Reference provenance

Before P2b is run, the provenance of any reference exponent is traced to a primary source and checked for overlap with the experiments entering the extraction. If the reference value derives wholly or partly from the same observations, P2b is reclassified as **DESCRIPTIVE** and reports \(\gamma\) with uncertainty without any recovery claim. A recovery claim requires a reference established on disjoint experiments, documented by citation in the ledger.

In particular, the approximately \(D^{-0.62}\) exponent identified during the literature audit is treated as **unverified for independence** until its underlying experimental provenance is resolved; it cannot serve as the recovery target by default.

### Dose-range truncation

The primary P2b regression is restricted to doses at or above the ID50 for the relevant strain–host–route combination, with the ID50 taken from independent published titration. Observations below the ID50 are analysed separately and never pooled into the primary fit because the dose–incubation relation is not assumed log-linear there. Where an ID50 is not independently available for a combination, that combination is excluded from the primary analysis.

## 8.4 P2c — Phase-specific violation

If compatible early- and late-phase kinetic summaries are available for the same rate-modifier ladder, fit separate scale effects:

\[
a_{early},\qquad a_{late}.
\]

Primary test:

\[
H_0:\log a_{early}-\log a_{late}=0.
\]

A reproducible phase-specific modifier would be a direct violation of a single global scalar clock for the full trajectory.

### Expected outcome

It is recorded in advance that P2c is likely to terminate in **NOT IDENTIFIABLE** under §14 because the published phase-1 observable (prion titre as a function of time, from serial bioassay titration) and the phase-2 observable (plateau duration to clinical onset) are not commensurable time-to-event summaries. If this occurs it is reported as a data-availability/commensurability result, not as evidence for or against phase-specific rate control. Constructing a commensurable phase-1 event-time summary from titre trajectories would require a mapping learned from the same data and is prohibited under §5.1.

## 8.5 Prion pooling prohibition

Data are not pooled across strain, route, host species, or endpoint definitions merely to increase sample size. Those factors define different modules unless an independent mapping justifies pooling.

## 9.2 Data required

For each disease, only published numerical data not requiring figure digitization are admitted to the primary dataset.

Preferred form:

\[
(q_i,A_i),
\]

where \(q_i\) is repeat length and \(A_i\) is age at onset.

Required metadata:

- repeat-sizing method;
- onset definition;
- cohort;
- sample size;
- whether values are individual-level, grouped, or regression summaries;
- relevant repeat interruptions if reported.

### Repeat-sizing harmonisation

Repeat-sizing method (fragment analysis versus sequencing) is mandatory metadata. No numerical correction is applied in the primary analysis. A prespecified sensitivity analysis applies a +1 repeat correction to fragment-analysis-derived values and re-runs the primary model; the correction magnitude is varied over the reported −1 to +4 range. If the LODO conclusion changes sign anywhere in that range, the result is reported as **SENSITIVE TO REPEAT-SIZING METHOD**.

## 9.3 Primary common-form model

The primary cross-disease model is the threshold-aligned form:

\[
M_{P1}:\quad \log A_{di}=\alpha_d+f(q_{di}-q_{0,d})+\epsilon_{di},
\]

where \(f\) is common across diseases, \(\alpha_d\) is the single permitted disease-specific scale on age at onset, and \(q_{0,d}\) is the disease-specific pathogenic repeat threshold.

\(q_{0,d}\) is **fixed from independent published genetics before any fitting** and is not estimated from age-at-onset data. The primary threshold ledger uses one harmonized published table of pathological repeat ranges (Gardiner et al.-type population table; PMCID PMC6563569):

| Disease | Primary \(q_{0,d}\) | Published pathological range used for preflight |
|---|---:|---:|
| SCA1 | 39 | 39–91 |
| SCA2 | 33 | 33–500 |
| SCA3 | 60 | 60–87 |
| SCA6 | 20 | 20–33 |
| SCA7 | 34 | 34–460 |
| SCA17 | 41 | 41–66 |
| HD | 36 | 36–121 |
| DRPLA | 48 | 48–93 |
| SBMA | 36 | 36–73 |

These values are used only as externally fixed alignment constants. Alternative published threshold definitions enter the sensitivity analysis of §9.9; no threshold is estimated from the AAO outcome.

The functional class for \(f\) is selected from a small preregistered candidate set motivated by published genetics models before comparative performance across diseases is inspected.

## 9.4 Unaligned comparator

The unaligned model

\[
M_{P1a}:\quad \log A_{di}=\alpha_d+f(q_{di})+\epsilon_{di}
\]

is retained as a secondary comparator only. It is explicitly **not identified as a transfer test where disease repeat supports do not overlap**. Any apparent performance difference between \(M_{P1}\) and \(M_{P1a}\) is interpreted as an alignment effect, never as biological evidence.

## 9.5 Regime-boundary alternative

Where an independently documented juvenile/adult or analogous boundary exists:

\[
M_{P3}:f(q)=
\begin{cases}
f_1(q), & q<q_c\\
f_2(q), & q\ge q_c
\end{cases}
\]

with \(q_c\) prespecified from independent literature where possible.

This directly tests symmetry breaking via C1.

## 9.6 Primary cross-disease test: leave-one-disease-out

0. **Support-overlap precondition.** Compute the fraction of held-out observations of disease \(d\) whose aligned repeat value \(q_{di}-q_{0,d}\) falls inside the convex hull of aligned repeat values contributed by the training diseases. Denote this \(\omega_d\). A disease enters the primary LODO analysis only if \(\omega_d\ge0.80\). Diseases with \(\omega_d<0.80\) are reported separately as **EXTRAPOLATION CASES** with \(\omega_d\) stated, and their results never contribute to the §13.2 success criterion in either direction. \(\omega_d\) is computed and frozen before any predictive error is examined.

**Pre-freeze range check.** Using only published pathological repeat ranges and the fixed primary thresholds above—not age-at-onset outcomes—the aligned-support preflight does not make the test impossible: the range-based overlap is 1.00 for eight diseases and approximately 0.91 for held-out SCA2. This is only a feasibility check. The formal \(\omega_d\) is recomputed from the actually extracted repeat values before outcome prediction is examined.

For each eligible disease \(d\):

1. fit the common function \(f\) using all other diseases;
2. freeze \(f\);
3. estimate only \(\alpha_d\) on a calibration subset of disease \(d\), defined as the \(k\) observations with the smallest aligned repeat value \(q_{di}-q_{0,d}\), with ties broken by ascending age at onset and, if still tied, by source order in the ledger. Set

\[
k=\max(5,\lceil0.10n_d\rceil),
\]

capped at \(0.25n_d\). If \(n_d<20\), disease \(d\) is reported as **UNDERPOWERED FOR LODO** and excluded from the §13.2 criterion in either direction. Here \(n_d\) means independent analysis observations usable for AAO prediction; a reported cohort size cannot substitute for unavailable individual/grouped \((q,A)\) observations. The calibration subset is excluded from predictive-error computation. No other parameter is refitted;
4. predict the remaining held-out observations from disease \(d\).

The primary quantity is held-out predictive error relative to:

- a disease-specific unconstrained model;
- a common-form model with more than one disease-specific degree of freedom.

The important question is not whether the pooled fit is visually good, but whether a common threshold-aligned form transfers to a disease not used to learn that form.

## 9.7 SCA6 stress test

Before fitting, SCA6 is labeled a mechanistically distinct candidate exception.

Because its external threshold is far below the other polyQ loci, SCA6 becomes a particularly stringent transfer test after threshold alignment: the question is whether the common \(f\) transfers to a locus whose pathogenic absolute-repeat scale lies below the usual thresholds of the other diseases once only the externally fixed \(q_{0,d}\) translation is allowed.

Two results are possible:

- common-form transfer succeeds despite the mechanistic distinction;
- common-form transfer fails as predicted.

SCA6 is never removed solely because it worsens the fit.

## 9.8 HD regime test

Within HD, compare the global common-form model with the prespecified two-regime alternative.

If scalar/common-form scaling is adequate in adult onset but fails in juvenile-onset ranges, this supports a **regime-limited symmetry**, not global scaling.

## 9.9 Prespecified confounds and sensitivity analyses

The following are named before outcome extraction:

1. **Ascertainment and truncation.** Cohort type (population registry, clinic series, family study) is recorded per source and entered as a stratification variable. A sensitivity analysis repeats LODO restricted to registry-based cohorts only. Where reported, the age distribution of unaffected carriers is recorded to assess right-truncation.
2. **Threshold definition.** The primary \(q_{0,d}\) values are fixed by the harmonized source in §9.3. Sensitivity analyses repeat the LODO using the lower/upper alternative threshold definitions found in a prespecified secondary genetics table/review set; these alternatives are recorded in the numerical ledger before AAO fitting.
3. **Onset definition.** Motor, cognitive, mixed and self-reported onset definitions are recorded and not pooled across incompatible definitions without a documented mapping.
4. **Interruption status.** Where reported, alleles with CAA/CAT interruptions are flagged and analysed in a sensitivity stratum, because interruptions can decouple nominal repeat length from progression timing.
5. **Repeat-sizing method.** The §9.2 repeat-sizing sensitivity is mandatory whenever fragment analysis and sequencing are mixed.

A LODO result that does not survive the registry-only sensitivity analysis is reported as **CONFOUNDED BY ASCERTAINMENT**, not as transfer.

# 11. Model comparison

## 11.1 Preferred evidence

Model comparison prioritizes out-of-sample predictive performance.

Where sample size permits:

- leave-one-biological-replicate-out or grouped cross-validation;
- leave-one-module-out;
- leave-one-disease-out for P3.

Information criteria may be reported as secondary diagnostics.

## 11.2 Complexity ordering

Models are ordered:

\[
M_0: \text{no shared structure}
\]

\[
M_1: \text{single scalar time rescaling}
\]

\[
M_2: \text{prespecified offset/threshold extension}
\]

\[
M_3: \text{phase-specific or shape-changing model}
\]

\[
M_4: \text{flexible warping / diagnostic only}.
\]

A more flexible model cannot be interpreted as evidence for UTRH merely because it aligns the data.

# 12. Failure criteria for scalar scaling

A scalar-scaling claim is considered in tension or rejected within the tested regime if one or more prespecified diagnostics show robust incompatibility, including:

1. corresponding quantile ratios vary systematically with quantile;
2. dimensionless shape statistics differ beyond uncertainty;
3. a shared Weibull/stretched-exponential shape parameter is inadequate;
4. early and late phases require materially different scale factors;
5. residuals show reproducible multimodality/frailty not captured by the scalar model;
6. a prespecified regime-specific model materially outpredicts the single-scale model;
7. a held-out disease cannot be predicted using the shared form plus the allowed scalar degree of freedom.

No single noisy Kaplan–Meier crossing is sufficient by itself.

---

# 13. Success criteria

## 13.1 Local temporal-scaling success

A dataset/regime earns the label:

**SCALAR SCALING ADEQUATE LOCALLY**

only when:

- the scale model passes prespecified lack-of-fit checks;
- held-out prediction is acceptable;
- shape diagnostics do not contradict scaling;
- a more flexible shape model does not yield a material, reproducible improvement.

**Triviality exclusion.** Define the preregistered near-exponential resolution as \(\delta_\beta=0.10\). If the estimated common shape parameter has a 95% interval wholly contained within \([0.90,1.10]\), the result is labelled **SHAPE TEST UNINFORMATIVE (NEAR-EXPONENTIAL REGIME)** rather than SCALAR SCALING ADEQUATE LOCALLY. In this regime, shape invariance is close to a family property and discrimination must rest on scale-relation tests (P2b, P3) or non-survival anchors. The alternative thresholds 0.05 and 0.15 are reported only as sensitivity analyses and cannot change the primary label.

## 13.2 Cross-disease success

The polyQ analysis earns:

**ONE-PARAMETER CROSS-DISEASE TRANSFER SUPPORTED**

only if the common function learned without disease \(d\) predicts disease \(d\) after the single allowed disease-specific scale calibration and this succeeds across a prespecified fraction of eligible diseases.

No pooled in-sample visual collapse can satisfy this criterion.

## 13.3 Conditional-symmetry success

The conditional theory is strengthened only if prespecified C1–C3 labels predict failures and successes better than a null classification that ignores those mechanisms.

This comparison is secondary in v1 because the number of mechanistically classified systems may be small.

# 14. Stopping rules

The analysis stops with **NOT IDENTIFIABLE** rather than relaxing criteria when:

- required summary statistics are not reported;
- event definitions are incompatible;
- biological replicate structure cannot be recovered;
- censoring cannot be reconstructed;
- a required transformation would have to be learned from the same outcome being tested;
- primary numerical extraction would require unauthorized or non-preregistered figure digitization;
- P2a observation granularity is inseparable from the rate modifier (VIF >5);
- the realised P2a design has MDE >0.45 on the standardized log-CV slope scale;
- a P3 disease fails the prespecified support-overlap or minimum-observation gate.

Failure to obtain enough data is a result, not permission to alter the model.

# 17. Interpretation matrix

| Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Scalar scaling adequate in benchmark and selected NDD-related regimes | Local temporal-scaling symmetry is empirically plausible at the tested level; not generator-level proof |
| Shape invariant but scale relation differs | Common shape may exist without the proposed rate-modifier law |
| Shape invariant with \(\beta^*\) in the preregistered near-exponential band | **Uninformative for discriminating M1:** near-exponential families are closed or nearly closed under one-parameter rescaling |
| Scale changes but dimensionless shape changes too | Not pure temporal rescaling |
| Early/late scale factors differ | Phase-specific rate control; violates a global scalar clock if the observables are commensurable |
| P2a MDE gate fails or granularity is inseparable from modifier | Uninformative / NOT IDENTIFIABLE; no null or anti-scaling interpretation |
| One-parameter LODO transfer works across polyQ diseases | Evidence for a transferable cross-disease common form at the population-genetic level. **Does not discriminate between a shared downstream generator and shared upstream nucleation/lag kinetics with locus-specific rate constants; both can predict common-form scaling.** |
| SCA6 fails as preregistered while canonical polyQ diseases transfer | Mechanistically delimited scaling class, conditional on sensitivity analyses |
| P3 transfer changes under registry-only or sizing/threshold sensitivity | Confounded/sensitive; not counted as transferable scaling |
| All eligible diseases require disease-specific shape | Cross-disease scalar common-form hypothesis rejected at this level |
| NDD cellular raw data remain unavailable | Strong cellular UTRH remains untested, not disproved |

# 18. Claims that are explicitly forbidden after v1.2 analyses

Regardless of numerical outcome, the following claims are not licensed by these analyses alone:

- “all neurodegenerative diseases share the same generator”;
- “the UTRH is proven”;
- “a common CV proves a common pathway”;
- “polyQ onset scaling proves cellular temporal scaling”;
- “polyQ LODO transfer identifies the shared generator” — the design cannot separate a shared downstream generator from shared upstream aggregation/nucleation kinetics;
- “prion incubation scaling generalizes to all NDDs”;
- “failure of one local AFT fit falsifies every possible restricted UTRH”.

The strongest allowed conclusion is conditional and level-specific.
